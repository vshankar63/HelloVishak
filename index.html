About Vishak

I've been a Senior PM at Mews for the last two and a half years, owning POS, so I already know the org, the people, and honestly, the friction this role is trying to solve from the inside. That's a big part of why I'm interested.

Before Mews I spent several years at product and program agencies. A lot of that work was less about building a single product and more about looking across a system, finding what was broken, and building the infrastructure to fix it. The clearest example is Goldman Sachs. I came in to find six or seven product teams (Marcus, Marquee, TxB) all running their own design systems, their own component libraries, no shared language. My job was to diagnose that, align the teams on a single approach, and build the governance that made it stick. That's essentially the same problem this role is solving, just applied to the product value stream rather than a design system.

More recently I've been leaning into AI heavily in my day to day. I built an automated self-onboarding tool at Mews that cut manual steps by 90%, and I've been running a product monthly to upskill PMs across the org. Both feel directly relevant to what this role needs.

How I See This Role

Reading the job description and knowing the direction that Mews is going, to me this role is a Product Builder for internal R&D functions in Mews. My customer in this case is Mews and my success metrics, outcomes, and goals all have this in mind.

North Star

Unify disparate processes and practices into a cohesive Mews R&D Operating System, enabling Mews' R&D to scale without losing development speed, code quality, or cross-company alignment.

Today's Problem

R&D and GTM are operating out of sync with each other rather than in step, and that's creating friction, confusion, and wasted effort as we grow.

How This Role Moves The Needle

Success of this role would result in R&D and GTM being more in alignment from discovery until post launch, rather than the current adversarial state of working.

3 Observations From The Inside

After working in Product on POS and leading initiatives across the ecosystem on topics like guest profiles, payments, onboarding, digital ordering, and billing, I've observed areas where the partnership between R&D and GTM hasn't worked as well as it should.

01

We Don't Speak The Same Language

What I See

The most immediate friction I've felt is that 'ready for launch' means completely different things depending on who you ask. To R&D it means production-ready (the code is shipped). To GTM it means the release notes are written, the help centre is updated, the sales team is briefed. Neither side is wrong, but because there's no shared definition, things fall through the gap every single time. The same problem shows up with terminology like GA, Pilot, Beta, and Closed Beta, with no agreed vocabulary meaning every team interprets it differently when planning.

Low-Hanging Fruit
  • Run a workshop or structured interviews with Comm and R&D teams on recent or upcoming product launches, mapping the full end-to-end process and the terminologies each side uses. From there, create a glossary of what R&D thinks something means and what GTM thinks it means.
  • Embed the aligned glossary into everyday tooling like Confluence and JPD tickets so it travels downstream automatically.
02

The Release Process Is Manual, Fragmented, And Lives In Too Many Places

What I See

When a feature ships, the process splits across at least three disconnected systems. JPDs live in the R&D roadmap and Jira. Product Marketing writes Confluence pages that reference those JPDs but aren't connected to them. Product Content has their own separate Jira process that nobody else feeds into, so all updates are made manually. The Tier system GTM put in place was a good attempt at creating structure, but because it isn't connected to where R&D does their work, it creates duplication and things get missed.

Low-Hanging Fruit
  • Using the output from the workshops or interviews, create (and compare to anything existing) a consistent launch checklist that includes clear task owners and a definition of done before anything goes live.
  • Manage this via an agent with direct automations between Jira, JPD, and Confluence so the checklist stays in sync without anyone having to update it manually.
03

GTM Is Too Far Downstream. Product Is Carrying Commercial Work It Shouldn't Be

What I See

Commercial teams feel accountable for GTM outcomes but aren't brought into discovery early enough to actually shape them. When it comes to commercial sizing of a new feature, it often falls to the PM to do that work, even though GTM counterparts have the customer relationships and market context to do it better. The signal GTM collects from pre-conception through to post-launch isn't being systematically fed back into the product knowledge base.

Low-Hanging Fruit
  • Introduce Product Discovery kickoff sessions as a formal part of the end-to-end cycle. The purpose is purely to explore the idea and define what we want to learn, with no commitment to specific development timelines.
  • Deploy an agent that listens across Mews for mentions of specific products (e.g. Digital Ordering surfacing during onboarding or prospect calls) and prepares monthly reports back to Product and GTM teams, with direct comments posted to the relevant JPD ticket.

90 Day Plan

What problems am I trying to solve?  |  Who for?  |  How do I measure success for myself and the system?  |  What manual steps can be automated today?

Across All 90 Days
  • Start documenting the AI infrastructure of agents and processes as it is built
  • Understand the Product Ops team OKRs and success metrics
  • Learn from existing Product Ops insights alongside conducting my own discovery
Phase 1

Listen & Map

Days 1–30
  • Build an interview guide for use with R&D and GTM teams across each department, so I can build relationships and have consistent data for my agents
  • From the first interview, use AI to build a living diagram that iterates with each additional session, calling out gaps, friction points, and language mismatches. This artifact builds trust with both sides and clearly shows what needs fixing first
Takeaways

Pain points, similarities in process, what works well, known gaps, language mismatches.

Phase 2

Synthesise & Ship

Days 31–60
  • Synthesise and socialise findings from the first 30 days with the Product Ops team, then R&D and GTM leadership
  • Ship a quick win: a glossary for language alignment embedded into tooling, or a standardised launch readiness model
  • Get GTM and R&D in one room to build a shared model for release from discovery through to 1 year post-launch
  • Use that model on an upcoming release so agents and skills can be built to remove manual tasks and collect feedback as the model is used
Takeaways

A working release model between GTM and R&D. A glossary for unified language. A feedback loop for both.

Phase 3

Systematise & Scale

Days 61–90
  • Launch the PM Guild
  • Build an agent to check usage metrics and solicit feedback on the launch readiness model and glossary from PMs who are using them and those who are not
  • Define and refine success metrics for the role
  • Start building a PM Onboarding skill that listens across Mews, iterates on existing documented process, and builds toward a full agent
  • Build additional quick wins surfaced during the first two months
  • Retrospective of the GTM and R&D model
Takeaways

Structure and cadence for PM Guild. PM Onboarding Flow outline. Usage metrics and agents for quick wins from the previous two months.

The sequence matters: diagnose carefully, ship a quick win to build credibility, then move into structural work.
